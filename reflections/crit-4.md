# Crit 4 — Pantheon

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Realising the agent could not hear what it had built.

Artemis passed everything. Her voice was constructed correctly, scheduled the
right pitches, leaked no nodes, and showed up in the measured output peak.
Forty-odd checks were green across three viewports. She still made no sound
when I tapped her, and I only knew because I played it.

That was not a hole in the coverage. Every check the agent could write measured
something adjacent to hearing — sources started, node counts, peak amplitude,
filter frequencies — and no number of extra checks would have crossed the gap
between "scheduled correctly" and "made a sound when touched."

So the work divided by what each of us could perceive. I supplied the signal
that something was wrong. The agent supplied the reason — Artemis is the only
god with no slot on step 0, so she was always at least 0.42 s late — and the
probe that proved the fix. Neither half was optional.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I have been treating myself as the agent's user. This week I stopped. A user
issues requests and accepts what comes back, and that is not what this work
needed. It needed a co-worker.

The division that emerged is one I want to keep. The agent implements and
fixes — it held seven voices and their node lifetimes at once, which I could
not. I audit and troubleshoot: I play the thing, judge whether it is right, and
say so.

That is not the easy half. To reject a wrong diagnosis I first had to have
tested it myself. Being the one who judges means knowing what good looks like,
so my own judgement matters more in this way of working, not less.
